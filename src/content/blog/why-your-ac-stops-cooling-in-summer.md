An air conditioner that stops cooling during a heat wave is stressful. Before assuming the worst, a few simple checks can identify easy fixes—or confirm that professional service is needed.

## Thermostat Settings

Confirm the thermostat is set to cool and the setpoint is several degrees below the current room temperature. If you have a programmable or smart thermostat, check that a schedule or hold setting is not overriding your intent.

## Air Filter

A severely clogged filter reduces airflow and can cause the indoor coil to ice up or the system to shut down on a safety limit. If the filter looks packed with dust, replace it and give the system time to recover.

## Outdoor Unit

Make sure the outdoor unit is running and free of leaves, grass, and debris. A unit that is not running at all may have a tripped breaker, a failed capacitor, or another electrical issue.

## Condensate Drain

Many systems shut off cooling when the condensate drain is clogged and the float switch trips. If you see water around the indoor unit, a clogged drain is a likely culprit.

## When to Call

If basic checks do not restore cooling, or if you hear unusual noises, smell burning, or see ice on the refrigerant lines, professional diagnosis is the next step.
