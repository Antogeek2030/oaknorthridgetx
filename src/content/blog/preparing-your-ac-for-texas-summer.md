Texas summers are long and demanding on air conditioning systems. Waiting until the first 95-degree day to discover a problem is avoidable. A little preparation helps systems start the season reliably.

## Schedule a Tune-Up

A spring maintenance visit typically includes cleaning, inspection of electrical components, and a performance check. Catching a weak capacitor, dirty coil, or restricted drain early is far less disruptive than a no-cooling call in July.

## Replace the Filter

Start the season with a clean filter and set a reminder for regular changes. Restricted airflow is one of the most common contributors to icing and limit trips.

## Test the System Before You Need It

Turn the AC on for a full cycle on a mild day. Confirm cool air at the registers, listen for unusual noises, and note any odd smells.

## Clear the Outdoor Unit

Keep at least two feet of clearance around the condenser. Trim vegetation and remove leaves and debris so airflow is not blocked.

## Know Your Breaker

Locate the breaker and outdoor disconnect for your AC. Knowing where these are saves time if service is needed.

Taking care of these basics does not guarantee a problem-free summer, but it does improve the odds. If you would like help preparing your system, [reach out](/contact/) to schedule a maintenance visit.
